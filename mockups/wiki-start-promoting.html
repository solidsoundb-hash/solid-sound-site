How to Start Promoting Yourself as a DJ: Your First 90 Days

You can mix. Friends say your sets are great. But no one is calling. Talent is the start, not the plan. Here is a simple 90-day plan that turns "a DJ with good sets" into "a DJ promoters know".

Quick answer: record one great 60-minute set, build a one-page press kit, post short clips every week, play every small gig you can in one scene, and send personal messages to 10 promoters a month. Keep it up for 90 days before you judge the results.

1Days 1–30: your calling card

  • One signature set. 60 minutes, your sound, recorded clean (no clipping, no red lights). This is what every promoter will hear first.
  • Your name and look. One DJ name, one photo, the same everywhere: Instagram, SoundCloud, email.
  • A one-page press kit (EPK). Photo, three-line bio, genre, the link to your set, past gigs, contacts. A PDF or a simple page.

2Days 31–60: be seen

  • Short clips, every week. 15–30 seconds of you mixing, a crowd moment or a track ID. Video beats photos.
  • Go out. Attend the nights you want to play. Talk to the residents and the promoter, without pitching straight away.
  • Play anywhere relevant. Bars, warm-ups, friends' parties, live streams. Every gig is a clip and a contact.

3Days 61–90: ask for gigs

  • 10 personal messages a month to promoters whose nights match your sound. Mention a night you attended, add your set and EPK, keep it under 100 words.
  • Bring people. When you get a slot, invite your crowd. Then tell the promoter how many came.
  • Start making tracks. Original music is the strongest long-term promotion a DJ has.

✓Checklist

  • One 60-minute set, recorded clean
  • Same DJ name and photo on every platform
  • One-page press kit with your set link
  • At least one short clip a week
  • 10 personal messages to promoters a month
  • Every gig: a clip, a photo, a thank-you message

?FAQ

How long until I get my first paid gig?

For most DJs who follow a plan like this, the first paid slots come within a few months. It depends on your city and how active the scene is.

Do I need my own tracks?

Not for your first gigs. To move up to bigger stages, original music makes the biggest difference.
