Techno Rumble Kick in Ableton: 6 Steps, Stock Devices Only

A deep, rolling low end under your kick, made only with Ableton's own devices. Works in Live 10, 11 and 12, Standard or Suite.

Short answer: send the kick to a return with a long, 100% wet Reverb, distort it with Saturator, cut everything above about 150 Hz and below 30 Hz with EQ Eight, then sidechain the rumble to the kick so it ducks on every hit and swells back in between. Mono it below 150 Hz.

I'm a mixing and mastering engineer, and I make techno in Ableton. A weak or muddy rumble is one of the most common problems I hear in demos. Below is the method I use, step by step, with numbers you can copy.

Step 1 — Start with a short, clean kick

The rumble is built from the kick, so the kick decides everything. Use a punchy kick with a short tail (about 150–300 ms) and a clear pitch. A long, boomy kick gives a messy rumble. Tune it to your track's key if you can: the rumble will follow.

Settings: kick tail 150–300 ms · fundamental around 45–60 Hz · kick peaks about −6 dB on its channel

Step 2 — Send the kick to a return with a huge reverb

Create a return track (Ctrl/Cmd+Alt+T), name it RUMBLE and put Reverb on it. Turn the send on the kick channel up. The rumble is the reverb tail, so make it long and dark.

Settings: Reverb: Dry/Wet 100% · Decay 3–6 s · Size 100 · Pre-delay 0–20 ms · Hi Shelf on and pulled down · Diffusion high · Stereo 0% (or narrow) · send from the kick about −10 to −6 dB

Prefer a darker, more "pumping" rumble? Put Reverb on an audio track that receives the kick, freeze or resample it and chop it later. The return-track version is faster to tweak.

Step 3 — Distort the tail

A clean reverb tail is too soft for techno. After Reverb, add Saturator (or Drum Buss with Drive) to thicken it and bring out harmonics, so the rumble is heard on small speakers too.

Settings: Saturator: Analog Clip or Soft Sine · Drive 6–15 dB · Output down to match level · Dry/Wet 100% · optional second Saturator for more grit

Step 4 — Carve the rumble with EQ Eight

Now keep only the low end. Put EQ Eight after the distortion and cut everything that isn't rumble. Too much top end turns into noise and fights your hats and synths.

Settings: EQ Eight: low cut (48 dB/oct) at 25–35 Hz · high cut (48 dB/oct) at 120–200 Hz · small dip of 2–4 dB at the kick fundamental (45–60 Hz) so kick and rumble don't stack

Step 5 — Sidechain the rumble to the kick

This is what makes it roll. Add Compressor at the end of the return, open the sidechain, choose the kick as the source. On every kick the rumble ducks, then swells back in between: that's the groove.

Settings: Compressor: Sidechain on, source = kick (Pre FX or Post FX) · Ratio 4:1–∞:1 · Attack 0.1–1 ms · Release 80–200 ms (to taste, by tempo) · Threshold so the rumble drops 6–12 dB on each hit

Alternative: an LFO tool or Auto Pan in a volume shape gives a more regular "pump" without depending on the kick level.

Step 6 — Mono, level and check

Low end must be mono, or it collapses on club systems and phones. Add Utility after the compressor and set Width to 0%, or use Bass Mono (Live 11 and later) at about 150 Hz. Then set the level against the kick.

Settings: Utility: Width 0% or Bass Mono 120–150 Hz · rumble 3–8 dB quieter than the kick on the meter · check in mono and on headphones

3 common mistakes

  • Kick and rumble on the same note at the same time. They stack and clip. Duck the rumble harder or dip the kick frequency in the rumble's EQ.
  • Stereo low end. Wide reverb below 150 Hz sounds big in headphones and disappears in a club. Mono it.
  • Rumble louder than the kick. The kick leads, the rumble follows. Mix it 3–8 dB under.

FAQ

What is a rumble kick in techno?

A kick followed by a dark, distorted low-end tail, usually made from a long reverb on the kick that is EQ'd, saturated and sidechained so it ducks on each hit and rolls between them.

Can I make a rumble with stock Ableton devices?

Yes. Reverb, Saturator, EQ Eight, Compressor and Utility come with Ableton Live Standard and Suite, so no third-party plugins are needed.

Why is my rumble muddy?

Usually too much 150–400 Hz, a stereo low end, or kick and rumble peaking at the same time. Cut above ~150 Hz, mono it and sidechain it harder.

How loud should the rumble be?

Roughly 3–8 dB quieter than the kick on the meter. Check on headphones, in mono and on a small speaker.
