How Much Does Mixing and Mastering Cost in 2026? Real Prices

What a fair price looks like, what you get at each level, and the extras that quietly double the bill.

Short answer: in 2026 a song mix costs about $50–150 from a budget engineer, $150–700 from an indie pro and $1,500+ from a name engineer. Mastering costs about $10 with AI, $25–75 from a budget engineer and $150–600 from an established one. Mix + master together is usually cheaper than booking them separately.

Step 1 — Decide what you actually need

Mixing balances your separate tracks (stems): levels, EQ, compression, space, effects. Mastering is the final polish of the finished stereo mix: tone, loudness and consistency across platforms. If your mix already sounds right and only lacks loudness and polish, you need mastering. If parts fight each other, the vocal sits wrong or the low end is muddy, you need mixing first. Not sure? A free review tells you in a day.

Rule of thumb: mix problems can't be fixed in mastering.

Step 2 — Count your tracks

Most engineers price mixing by the number of stems. 2–8 stems (beat + vocal, simple electronic track) sits at the low end of a tier. 30–50+ stems (live band, big orchestral or layered EDM) can cost two or three times more from the same engineer. Bounce groups you're happy with (for example, all drums to one stereo stem) to save money, but keep the main vocal and bass separate.

Numbers: under 10 stems — base price · 10–30 — mid · 30+ — top price or custom quote.

Step 3 — Check what the price includes

Two quotes of $150 can be very different deals. Look for:

  • Revisions: 2–3 rounds is normal. Extra rounds often cost extra.
  • Mastering included? Many mix prices include a basic master. A separate master costs more.
  • Files: streaming master, instrumental, clean version, stems. Each one can be an add-on.
  • Turnaround: rush delivery usually adds 25–50%.
  • Vocal tuning and editing: often priced per track, not included in the mix.

Step 4 — Listen before you pay

Price says little about fit. Ask for before/after examples in your genre and listen on headphones and a phone. A techno engineer and a hip-hop engineer hear the low end differently. Many engineers offer a short test or a free review: use it. Red flags: no examples, no revision policy, "we can make it as loud as any record" with no talk about your mix.

Step 5 — Budget the whole release

For a single: mix + master from an indie pro usually lands around $200–850. For an EP of 4–5 songs, ask for a package price: engineers often discount 10–20% for several tracks. Leave 10–15% of your budget for extras like an instrumental, a clean version or one more revision round.

3 common mistakes

  • Paying for mastering to fix a bad mix. Mastering polishes; it can't rebalance a vocal or a muddy kick and bass.
  • Comparing prices, not packages. Revisions, files and turnaround decide the real price.
  • Sending messy files. Unlabelled stems, different start points or clipped exports cost you time and money. Export every stem from bar 1, same length, 24-bit WAV, no limiter on the master bus.

FAQ

How much should I pay to mix and master one song?

For an independent release, about $200–850 for mix and master from an indie pro, or $75–225 from a budget engineer. Name engineers start around $1,500 for the mix alone.

Is AI mastering good enough?

For demos and quick references, often yes: it costs about $10 per song or a monthly plan. For a release, a human engineer catches mix problems AI can't fix and adapts the master to your genre.

Why do mixing prices vary so much?

Experience and demand, the number of stems, revisions, turnaround and extra files. Two quotes at the same price can include very different work.

Should I book mixing and mastering separately?

A second pair of ears on the master is a real benefit, but booking both from one engineer is usually cheaper and faster. For a first release, a package is the simpler choice.
