How to Record Clean Vocals at Home: 6 Steps, No Vocal Booth

A dry, close, release-ready vocal from a normal bedroom, with the gear you already have.

Short answer: sing 10–15 cm (one hand span) from the mic, with the mic beside your mouth at lip level, turned 20–30° and a pop filter 5–8 cm in front. Set the gain so your loudest line peaks at −10 to −6 dBFS, then keep the exact same position for every take.

I'm a mixing and mastering engineer, and most home vocals I get don't need a better mic. They need these six steps. Each one takes a minute or two and costs nothing.

Step 1 — Put something soft behind you

A cardioid mic hears mostly what's in front of it: you, and the wall behind your head. That wall matters more than the one behind the mic. Stand with your back to a wardrobe full of clothes, a full bookshelf or a duvet on a stand, and face the open room, not a corner or a window.

Settings: soft surface 30–100 cm behind you · open room in front.

Step 2 — Sing one hand span from the mic

Thumb on your chin, little finger on the pop filter: that's 10–15 cm. Your direct voice follows the inverse square law: halve the distance and it gets about 6 dB louder, while the room's reflections hardly change. From 60 cm to 15 cm your voice is about 12 dB louder against the same room. A €3,000 condenser at arm's length records more room than a €100 dynamic mic at a hand span.

Closer than about 5 cm, the proximity effect makes the bass muddy and every breath loud. Further than 50 cm, you record the room, and no plugin removes it cleanly later.

Settings: 10–15 cm for most vocals · see the table in Step 4 for your style.

Step 3 — Turn the mic 20–30° and add a pop filter

Every P, B, T and K pushes a jet of air straight ahead. Your voice spreads in all directions, the air doesn't. Put the mic beside your mouth at lip level, turn it 20–30° toward you, and the air flies past the capsule. The angle also softens harsh S sounds, so you need less de-essing later. Point the back of the mic at the noisiest thing in the room: window, computer fan or bare wall.

Settings: mic at lip level · 20–30° off-axis · pop filter 5–8 cm from the capsule.

30-second test: say "Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers" straight into the mic, then again with the mic turned 25°. Watch the meter: the thumps are gone and your voice sounds the same.

Step 4 — Match the distance to your mic and style

A dynamic mic like the Shure SM7B or SM58 hears much less of the room, so it works best close. A large-diaphragm condenser like the Neumann TLM 103 captures more detail and air, and more room too, so keep the hand span and the soft wall behind you. Condensers need 48V phantom power.

StyleDynamic micCondenser mic
Whisper, intimate pop5–8 cm5–10 cm
Rap, spoken word5–10 cm10–15 cm
Pop, R&B, ballads8–12 cm10–15 cm
Belting, rock, big choruses10–15 cm15–25 cm

Noisy, untreated room? Start with a dynamic. Quiet room and soft vocals? A condenser, used close.

Step 5 — Set the gain on your loudest line

Don't set the gain on the intro. Sing the loudest line of the song and turn the gain up until it peaks around −10 to −6 dBFS, then leave it. In 24-bit that leaves plenty of headroom and the noise floor stays far below your voice. A dynamic mic like the SM7B needs a lot of clean gain, so check your audio interface for vocals first.

Settings: 24-bit, 44.1 or 48 kHz · peaks −10 to −6 dBFS · closed-back headphones at a comfortable level, so the click doesn't leak into the mic.

Step 6 — Lock your position for every take

Move 10 cm between takes and the tone and level change: comping turns into a puzzle and doubles never sit together. Use a solid stand or boom arm with a shock mount, tape your spot on the floor, and touch the pop filter lightly with your nose before each take, then lean back. Keep the same distance, angle and gain for verses, choruses, doubles and ad-libs. Recording over several days? Take a photo of the setup.

Consistent doesn't mean frozen: lean back a few centimetres on the loudest notes and come in a little on soft lines. That keeps the vocal even before any compressor.

3 common mistakes

  • Buying gear first. A new mic at the wrong distance still sounds like a bathroom. Fix the distance, then decide.
  • Foam on the wall behind the mic. The wall behind you is the one the mic hears.
  • Hoping the mix will fix it. A mix can fix tone, dynamics, sibilance and pitch. It can't cleanly remove a distant, roomy sound or a plosive that clipped.

Checklist before you press REC

  • Soft things behind you, open room in front
  • Mic 10–15 cm from the mouth (closer on a dynamic)
  • Mic beside the mouth, at lip level
  • Turned 20–30°, pop filter 5–8 cm in front
  • Back of the mic pointed at the noise
  • Gain set on the loudest line, peaks −10 to −6 dBFS
  • Spot marked on the floor, stand and shock mount tight
  • Same position and gain for every take and double

FAQ

How far should the mic be from your mouth when singing?

About 10–15 cm, one hand span, for most vocals. Go closer (5–10 cm) for whispers, rap or a dynamic mic, and back off to 15–25 cm for loud belting on a condenser.

How do I stop popping sounds on my vocals?

Use a pop filter 5–8 cm in front of the mic, turn the mic 20–30° off-axis and keep at least 10 cm of distance. A high-pass filter in the mix helps with what's left.

Why does my vocal recording sound echoey?

You're too far from the mic, so the room's reflections are almost as loud as your voice. Move to a hand span and put soft things behind you, not behind the mic.

Can I record good vocals without a vocal booth?

Yes. Singing close into a cardioid mic with soft things behind you keeps the room low enough for a release. Billie Eilish's debut album was recorded in a bedroom.
